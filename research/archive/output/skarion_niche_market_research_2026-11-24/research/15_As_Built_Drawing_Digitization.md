# As-built and record drawing digitization

**Reachable demand 4/5, medium-high confidence. Supply tightness 2/5 for open conversion work, medium-high confidence, and 3/5 in the small sample of controlled federal buying, medium confidence.** Buyers repeatedly purchase redraw and document conversion, with specialist providers, offshore marketplace proposals, and in-house software all competing for the simpler work. Skarion has high access to private, client-directed production using approved existing files, while the capacity of vetted private suppliers for complex, standards-specific batches remains unknown.

## Reachable offer

Redraw a batch of existing scanned PDF/TIFF records or update a buyer-supplied DWG from approved construction redlines. Deliver editable native CAD, plotted PDFs, a revision index, and an exceptions log for missing dimensions, damaged sheets, or conflicting revisions. Target facility drawing managers, utilities, MEP and industrial engineering primes, and architecture or construction firms with funded archive cleanup, renovation, or closeout batches. Distinguish simple vector-PDF extraction, full redraw of raster scans, and redline reconciliation in the quote. Pay for complex cleanup is plausible because Autodesk explains that ordinary PDF import cannot vectorize raster content, and buyers such as Merced specify native editable geometry and can reject raster attachments. [Autodesk PDF import guidance](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2026/ENU/AutoCAD-Core/files/GUID-1202CC8A-364F-4E93-8E86-6F476CD83C72.htm) · [Merced specification, pp. 5–6](https://www.cityofmerced.gov/home/showpublisheddocument/24044/639076080283730000)

Qualify what the buyer takes responsibility for. The existing record and approved changes define the drawing basis. New measurement, site validation, licensed design and sign-off, and decisions about what was actually constructed belong to the qualified owner or prime. The as-built label alone can hide any of those tasks. A construction project requiring electronic record drawings establishes a deliverable in a project workflow. Count it as purchasable desktop work only when the owner buys it directly or the prime authorizes a separable production package.

## Decision and next test

Use this as a **specific offer within the drafting campaign**. The same federal buying and archive observations support both high-demand scores. A stand-alone top-five slot needs stronger private evidence of a recurring backlog current suppliers cannot clear. The strongest plausible distinction is revision-controlled batch production for a facility or engineering owner with a defined drawing standard and repeat trigger. In an initial screen, prioritize currently funded renovations, records migrations, and closeout lots, then ask who handles redraw, how many sheets enter the queue each month, and whether supplier turnaround or first-pass acceptance delays the work.

Propose a paid **three-to-five-sheet pilot** for one owner’s discipline and version. Accept a representative clean and difficult source, an approved markup list, a drawing template, and confirmed scale controls. Quote by sheet class after inspection. Deliver DWG, plotted PDF, source-to-output revision register, and open questions. Have the buyer accept agreed scales and units, layer and title-block conformance, independent dimension checks, and completeness against their redlines. Time drafter and checker hours separately. The decisive result is a paid second batch at workable margin with an approved data-sharing and QA workflow.
