# 04 Stormwater asset mapping and engineering production support

**Research cutoff November 24, 2026 · United States**

Municipalities are actively buying stormwater inventories, as-built reconciliation, and drainage system updates, both as standalone mapping contracts and within engineering studies. A November 2026 Michigan mapping competition attracted 10 firms, while published records establish a path for GIS teaming and leave the supply of qualified desktop-only subcontractors unknown.

| Dimension | Rating | Confidence and scope |

## Offer and reachable buyers

Sell an **editable drainage inventory with source references and a model-input exception register**. The primary buyer is the GIS lead, stormwater project manager, or asset management lead at an engineering prime with a funded municipal study or an active maintenance work order. Start with buyer-furnished plans, existing GIS, and approved inspection records. Reconcile IDs and geometry against source records, flag likely duplicates and unconnected structures, and provide the prime with a versioned data package. The prime retains field verification, acceptance of hydraulic inputs, model calibration, compliance judgments, and engineering sign-off.

Reachable demand requires evidence of a buying event and a plausible approval route for the bounded production scope. A city grant, permit, or capital plan is a trigger for prospect research. A prior awardee with upcoming municipal workload or an on-call contract becomes a qualified prospect when its team can identify a specific backlog and accepts the delivery model. Repeated catchment surveys, new projects added to the municipal system, ongoing permit mapping, and phased asset management plans can create follow-on batches. EPA guidance gives outfall and receiving-water mapping as a core MS4 activity. The Massachusetts permit illustrates annual updates as additional Phase II mapping information becomes available. Confirm the applicable local permit and whether the operator contracts for updates. [EPA municipal stormwater overview](https://www.epa.gov/npdes/stormwater-discharges-municipal-sources) · [Massachusetts MS4 permit, §2.3.4.5](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2025-11/final-2016-ma-sms4-gp-mod.pdf)

## Top five implications and validation

Stormwater warrants a conditional outreach campaign alongside utility GIS. The top-five case depends on evidence that primes have outsourced records work or expect to do so soon. Several credible firms competed for programs with similar mapping work, and a separately comparable supply denominator for desktop production remains unavailable. Count a contact once across general GIS, stormwater, and culvert inventory unless separate scopes or procurement decisions are verified.

The highest-value validation is a buyer-confirmed comparison from three or more regional stormwater primes covering a named upcoming project, who currently does GIS reconciliation, available internal capacity, and whether the prime would procure an approved pilot. A buyer confirming a first paid batch against its own schema is stronger evidence than a large public pipeline or a small prime bidder count. Suitable first targets for validation include firms named on Charlotte's proposal sheet or a prequalified engineering team with an active municipal work order. Qualify the proposed delivery location, data handling, subcontract consent, review capacity, schema, and reference requirements project by project.

**Paid pilot for scoping.** Take up to 100 buyer-supplied storm sewer features in one drainage area and an agreed set of source drawings. Deliver mapped source IDs, an editable geodatabase in the buyer's coordinate reference system, status fields for inferred and verified records, pipe endpoint and outfall exceptions, and a handoff manifest for the prime’s modeler. Include a shared decision log for disputed elevations or connections. Agree acceptance checks and review milestones before quoting. Track sampled attribute accuracy against source documents, unresolved exceptions, delivery effort, review and rework hours, and handoff acceptance. The prime directs all field and engineering verification.
