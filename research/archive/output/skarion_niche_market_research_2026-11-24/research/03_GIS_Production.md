# 03 GIS data cleanup and conversion

Research cutoff November 24, 2026 · United States

**Bottom line.** Municipalities are buying real, recurring GIS data maintenance, and ArcMap retirement has created additional migration and reconciliation work. Broad municipal GIS bids and general freelance digitizing show substantial competition. A narrow file-based production package sold through an established GIS prime is a better Skarion entry point, with supplier availability in that exact channel still unmeasured.

| Dimension | Rating | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reachable demand | **4/5** | Medium-high for underlying demand, medium for prime subcontract access. The records show repeated buying across markets and separable tasks. |
| Qualified supply tightness | **2/5 for general offers** | Medium for visible prime competition. Direct white-label capacity is **unknown**. |

## Offer and repeat trigger

Offer a bounded batch of GIS digitizing, schema conversion and cleanup from buyer-supplied files. Start with municipal GIS consultants, cadastral and planning firms, and implementation primes. Examples include zoning, address, easement or planning layers flowing from tabular or CAD inputs into a specified file geodatabase. A recurring trigger is routine county data imports, changes to municipal layers, or repeated production after a schema migration. The buyer retains authority over survey geometry and legal boundaries and approves deployment to production.

Buyer reach means the agency invites the relevant scope either directly or through a prime with room and permission to buy production capacity. Board agendas prove spending intent or selection where explicitly recorded. They rarely show the portion available to a subcontractor. Keep utility network production for water/wastewater and drainage or culvert inventories in their own niche counts.

## Decision and validation

**Retain as a high-demand candidate, with weak evidence for a supply shortage.** For a top-five list focused on both demand and scarcity, promote a specific GIS package only after a prime confirms the backlog, work split and limited qualified alternatives. The city data show a wide field for generic GIS, a narrower field for specialist platform integration, and a precedent for a mapping subconsultant in La Mirada. They do not measure unused specialist capacity.

The smallest defensible test is one layer or a sampled batch with buyer-specified coordinate system, geometry rules, source identifiers, attribute schema, acceptance checks and exclusions. Deliver editable data, source-to-target crosswalk and an exception log. Start with a representative 50–100 features as a proposed test size, then price recurring batches after measuring editing, review and corrections. Set the client’s GIS lead as final approver and clarify access before receiving any data.

The highest-value follow-up is to qualify 10 municipal GIS primes or regional GIS consultancies against actual recent scopes. Ask which routines they have delegated before, whether buyer terms allow remote production, batch sizes, their current turnaround or backlog, and whether they would fund a paid test. Document available alternatives and reasons for rejecting them. Use actual bids for the same deliverable or buyer-reported vendor coverage to test the supply hypothesis.
