# Pole attachment and make-ready records

**Demand 4/5, medium confidence. Supply tightness 3/5 for bundled audits and joint-use services, medium confidence. Exact remote subcontract supply is unknown. Accessibility is medium through an authorized utility vendor.** Utilities buy recurring attachment inventory, permit support, and records reconciliation, with the desk work explicitly identifiable in several scopes. Five observed competitions for wider scopes drew five to eight submissions, and established firms already provide integrated field and records services.

## Offer and buyer

Offer an **attachment records reconciliation and photo QA batch** to a joint-use program manager, attachment permit administrator, ISP deployment prime, or electric utility audit vendor. Inputs are the buyer’s supplied pole IDs, dated photos, field annotations, inventory and permit export, and classification rules. Return a source-linked record for each pole, draft attachment rows, an exception list for mismatched IDs or unverified ownership, and a reviewable GIS or spreadsheet import. Populate make-ready tracking status from approved records. Refer survey gaps, clearance or loading judgments, attachment ownership disputes, program decisions, and final postings to the buyer’s authorized personnel. Existing field records remain the source of measurements. [Example utility GIS and submission requirements](https://assets.centerpointenergy.com/api/public/content/indiana-pole-attachment-guidelines.pdf) · [ArcGIS Communications Pole Audit](https://doc.arcgis.com/en/arcgis-solutions/latest/reference/introduction-to-communications-pole-audit.htm)

The repeat trigger is a flow of returning field batches, new permits, transfers, or completion photos. Systemwide revenue audits can recur much less frequently. The prime route is more plausible than selling a whole audit direct because the records task is embedded in assignments with field measurements, qualified personnel, and owner-controlled systems. Under the federal rules that apply to its projects, the May 2026 FCC effective-date notice adds urgency around application completeness, timelines, and contractor approval. The notice provides workflow context. Actual purchase evidence follows below. [FCC effective-date notice](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-05-07/pdf/2026-09083.pdf)

## Decision and paid test

Keep this as a **top-five validation candidate tied to an approved prime**, especially for high-volume photo and record queues. Strong 2026 buyer scopes and repeat paid audits justify testing demand. The five published competition counts are for bundled buying across three utilities, with two observations from JEA’s recurring audit. The supplier evidence supports specialization and incumbent capacity, with private production congestion still unmeasured.

Propose one paid, fixed-scope **50-pole supplied-record batch** with a separate exception list, data dictionary, and reviewer handoff. Agree on matching rules, accepted source evidence, authorized users and environment, and the authorized reviewer before committing. Sample straightforward records and known ambiguous ones. Use the buyer’s validated results to measure accepted rows per production hour, independent reviewer minutes, correction rate, unresolved-exception rate, and next-batch volume. Keep pricing contingent on these inputs. Prime award and bid amounts include other work.

Ask a buyer who can provide an authorized batch about the backlog’s volume and age. Find out whether staff or existing vendors can clear it on time and whether the prime can authorize a separate desktop subcontract. A repeat purchase and observed turnaround from available vendors would provide evidence that Skarion can fill the queue profitably.
