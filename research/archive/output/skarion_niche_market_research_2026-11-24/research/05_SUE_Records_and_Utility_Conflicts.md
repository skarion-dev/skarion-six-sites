# Subsurface utility records and conflict drafting

**Research cutoff November 24, 2026.** Transportation buyers repeatedly require existing-utility drawings and conflict registers, and public records show how those deliverables enter wider engineering contracts. Public prime competitions attracted substantial supplier interest, while capacity and pricing for the narrower desktop work remain unmeasured.

| Dimension | Rating | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reachable demand for records and drafting support | **3/5** | Medium. Repeated, named requirements and a documented precedent for teaming on the larger work package. Limited visibility into separate purchases of desktop production. |
| Supply tightness | **Unknown for desktop production**. **2/5 for broader utility-coordination prime competitions.** | Low for exact subcontract capacity. Medium-high for the observed prime competitions. |

## Offer and buyer

Pitch **utility record reconciliation and conflict exhibit production** to a roadway designer, local transportation engineering consultant, or SUE firm with a funded corridor and an active utility coordination lead. Assemble client-authorized utility plans, permits, as-builts, and existing survey or field investigation outputs in a source register. Draft a composite CAD or GIS overlay and cross-reference each reviewer-identified issue to a source document, station, and feature ID. Carry the prime's existing classifications through unchanged and flag inconsistencies for review. The prime remains responsible for investigations, professional interpretation, quality-level determinations, actual conflict disposition, and the approved deliverable. [FHWA workflow](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/programadmin/history.cfm) · [ASCE 38-22 scope](https://sp360.asce.org/personifyebusiness/Merchandise/Product-Details/productId/280994734)

A buyer is reachable when a design or utility lead can assign and fund a desktop work package, grant access to the supplied records, and name a technical reviewer. Project milestones and changes in owner records or approved alignments can trigger repeat batches. Target the funded prime or task-order holder in a specific state. The buyer's platform and qualifications matter. MaineDOT, for example, specifies Bentley/MicroStation/OpenRoads for CADD work, and Minnesota DOT's published SUE prequalification calls for a Minnesota-licensed civil engineer, compatible CADD, and field and survey equipment. [MaineDOT consultant procurement](https://www.maine.gov/dot/doing-business/consultant-procurement) · [MnDOT SUE work type](https://www.dot.state.mn.us/consult/prequal/worktype/wt15-1.pdf)

## Top-five implication

**Keep as a targeted discovery candidate through primes.** Multiple buyer records support repeat need for the production artifacts, with the strongest fit where a designer has several active projects and conflicting legacy utility records. The public evidence supports neither a desktop supplier shortage nor an observable selling price for the exact service. The highest-value next fact is a task-order holder's recent use of an approved desktop subcontractor, the number of vetted alternatives they can call, and their paid rate or recent quote range for the same deliverables.

Test one paid records-and-exhibit batch covering a bounded corridor or intersection from supplied documents. Agree a client-approved CAD/GIS template, maximum input volume, data access, milestone and review owner before quoting. Deliver a source-linked register, one editable exhibit, the corresponding draft issue rows, and an exception log. Compare elapsed time and senior review effort with the buyer's current method. Scope further revisions against actual input quality and first-pass acceptance.
