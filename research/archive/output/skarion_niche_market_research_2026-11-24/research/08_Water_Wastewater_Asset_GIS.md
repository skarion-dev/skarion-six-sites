# 08 Water and wastewater asset GIS

**November 24, 2026. Demand 4/5 with high confidence that utilities buy the work and medium confidence in reach through primes. Supply tightness 3/5 with medium confidence for public prime competitions, and unknown for small desktop production subcontracts. Accessibility is medium through an approved US prime and conditional for a direct utility award.**

Utilities buy both migration and recurring records maintenance. Detroit bought recurring as-built QA and editing, and JEA approved a utility network program that explicitly assigns its water and wastewater workstream to a subcontractor. Prime competitions in the reviewed sample drew 3–8 bids or proposals across four 2025–2026 observations, with a historically thinner field for a narrowly defined production contract. [Detroit as-needed GIS award](https://dwsd.legistar.com/LegislationDetail.aspx?FullText=1&GUID=B03CFC69-29C8-460E-BA01-8FAD409AC75F&ID=7294137&Options=&Search=) · [JEA approval](https://www.wip.jea.com/Pdf/Download/12884943963) · [JEA scope](https://www.jea.com/Pdf/Download/12884943928)

## Service and buyer

Offer to reconcile supplied record drawings, construction submittals and field books against one existing water distribution or sanitary collection layer. Return a staged geodatabase or approved change set, mapped IDs and attributes, a document index, and an exception register for connectivity, geometry and uncertain records. A utility GIS lead or qualified integrator supplies the schema, source requirements and acceptance rules and approves production updates. A separate package can validate a source-to-target mapping, build reproducible checks and retest after correction in a test environment. The prime owns network design, enterprise deployment, live editing approval and field verification.

The initial buyer is a US water infrastructure consultant or GIS integrator with an awarded utility project and an identified production backlog. A direct buyer is plausible where the utility buys mapping or records QA as its own scope and Skarion meets the applicable requirements. Demand can recur as construction closeouts arrive, additional neighborhoods or networks are migrated, and GIS teams apply accepted changes to the system of record. Count utility awards as evidence of demand and count individual Skarion-accessible leads only when the workstream, responsible prime or buyer, and permission to subcontract are identifiable.

## Decision and test

Advance wet-utility records QA to top-five validation. It has a stronger buyer-specific proposition than general GIS, plus records that independently establish repeat buying, direct production procurement and a named subcontract pathway. A good buyer cohort comprises US integrators with a won migration, geography or network phase starting, and civil/water consultants who repeatedly turn as-builts over to clients. Build the campaign around quality and acceptance of staged updates. For lead-service-line inventories, use research niche 10 and treat a utility's distinct purchases and shared budget consistently to avoid duplicate demand.

Start with a fixed scope of **one district or 50–100 assets in one layer** using a buyer-approved sample and one or two source types. Agree on any tolerances, topology checks, permitted corrections, use of stable IDs, and reviewer acceptance before quoting. Deliver a changed-feature list with source references, counts by exception, before-and-after rule results, and issues requiring buyer decision. Keep unresolved infrastructure attributes marked unknown. Use a named ArcGIS reviewer, a client-authorized workspace and only data the client and prime allow Skarion to access in its proposed delivery locations. Seek written agreement on workstream ownership before proposing access to an active public project. WSSC, for example, restricts its engineering records portal to applicants with a substantiated business need. [WSSC access terms](https://www.wsscwater.com/work-with-us/codes-standards-policies-and-procedures/engineering-recordsinformation-weri)

The highest-value validation is to establish whether integrators with visible backlog already have spare records capacity or would pay for a bounded test. Capture how many of the researched primes have a live backlog, will allow external production, can give a usable schema and test extract, and will purchase QA separately. Record the time to provision access, reviewer hours per 100 features and approved corrections per reviewer hour. The approved pilot and first repeat batch would provide better evidence of accessible demand and qualified supply than counts of platform partners.
