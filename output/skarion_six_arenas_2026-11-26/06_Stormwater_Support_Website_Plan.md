# Skarion Stormwater | Website and 30-day pilot plan

**For team review · November 26, 2026**\
**Working brand** Skarion Stormwater · **Proposed page** `/services/stormwater-support` · **Stage** 30-day pilot

## Offer and buyer

Lead with **stormwater GIS inventory and field-record QA for engineering and inspection primes**. Start with firms that already hold the design, field, or asset management role and need surge production. Their buyer is the drainage project manager, GIS lead, or field operations lead who has to reconcile plans, exports, photos and CCTV records before the asset file reaches the owner. Good initial buyers include municipal drainage consultants, culvert field teams, and CCTV inspection firms already delivering to public owners.

**Suggested website headline**\
Stormwater asset inventory and GIS production support

**Suggested supporting copy**\
Turn drainage plans, field photos and inspection records into a traceable GIS inventory your team can review and import. Start with one basin or a sample of your backlog.

**Primary CTA** Request an inventory pilot. **Secondary CTA** View a sample delivery package.

### Offer sequence

| Order | Package | Customer inputs | Reviewable outputs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Stormwater GIS inventory sample | One basin, work order or buyer-selected batch of 25–50 features. CAD, GIS exports, as-built sheets or field tables. Owner schema, coordinate system and required fields. | Storm structures and pipe features or culvert points in the agreed format. Asset and source crosswalk, attribute dictionary, map PDF and validation summary. An exception log identifies duplicates, source conflicts, unresolved connectivity and missing evidence. |
| 2 | Field-source QA and closeout | Prime-approved field forms, GPS extracts, inspection reports, footage index, photos and GIS asset export. | Asset-to-record crosswalk, orphan and duplicate report, clearly identified record counts and a photo/CCTV relationship table. Owner-approved field values and condition codes retain their source and author. |
| 3 | Culvert and drainage structure register | Prime field collection or authoritative records, owner-assigned IDs, photographs and client condition schema. | Culverts, pipes, inlets, outfalls and associated structures at the agreed scope. Source-based fields, documented assumptions, issues queued by owner action, and batch release note. |
| 4 | Model input preparation after pilot | Prime-authored model schema and requirements, approved geometry and elevations, engineering lead and acceptance process. | GIS exports and a missing-input matrix for the prime's modeler. The qualified engineer owns hydrologic/hydraulic analysis, determinations and any sealed deliverables. |

The team should agree in writing on record count, feature types, coordinate reference, units, geospatial accuracy documented in the source, destination system, field rules, one review round, and scope for any remobilization. Price the sample after a source preview. Quote production by batch when the sample reveals effort per feature and usable source coverage. Keep field verification with the prime or a qualified survey or inspection partner under a separate scope. Retain exact field source for each elevation or condition observation and flag gaps for follow-up.

## Team workflow and measures

| Timing | Owners and actions | Evidence at review |
|---|---|---|
| Days 1–7 | Delivery and GIS leads choose a deliverable schema, run the three mock builds, and define how to point from a feature to its source. Brand and web owner reviews ten competitor websites using the register. | Test package, QA log and page draft with tested format claims. Record setup and rework time for each sample. |
| Days 8–14 | GIS reviewer independently validates sample layers. Web and marketing team prepare the landing page and first two original post drafts. Operations configures inquiry tags and sends a test form to the project queue. | A verified sample import, one documented reviewer and a readable page in the team’s internal preview. |
| Days 15–21 | Business development qualifies whether at least five prime-side conversations are possible and records their actual backlog, owner system, scope and decision timing. Offer a paid sample when requested. | Source constraint, market fit and a disposition for each eligible lead. |
| Days 22–30 | Delivery and sales leads review technical QA with actual or approved test files, pilot acceptance, effort, and whether a prime has follow-on volume. | Paid pilot decision, repeat-scope evidence if available, actual review minutes and production cost. Decide whether to scale, narrow the offer or continue learning. |

Suggested go/no-go measures are internal thresholds to confirm at kickoff. Require source or exception traceability for every delivered feature and zero critical import or schema errors in the agreed validator. Measure median minutes per feature, first-pass acceptance, percent with missing required attributes, original exceptions resolved, turnaround, reviewer time and cost per deliverable. A paid trial accepted by the buyer and a credible follow-on request justify expanding the effort. A reviewed demo with qualified conversations justifies collecting more demand evidence. Use website events `sample_open`, `pilot_form_start`, and `pilot_form_submit` alongside CRM stages for qualified buyer, scoped pilot and accepted package.

Release checklist for the delivery team:

- Confirm each dataset’s owner, date, coordinate reference and field units. Retain immutable versions of provided files and source IDs when the client allows.
- Count features by class, reconcile relationship keys, and run uniqueness, geometry and schema checks. Reconcile delivered count, quarantined records and unresolved exceptions to the input manifest.
- Let the prime choose the connectivity, elevation and inspection standards. Record any source transformation and the person responsible for acceptance.
- Check that public sample images are synthetic or approved for publication and that proposed claims reflect the company’s current ability to deliver and review.
