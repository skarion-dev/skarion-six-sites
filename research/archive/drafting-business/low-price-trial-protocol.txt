SKARION DRAFTING - LOW PRICE VALIDATION PROTOCOL
November 12, 2026

Purpose: test whether low-cost or free entry tasks lead to profitable repeat work.
This revises the earlier assumption that every pilot must have a 40% margin.
No campaign has been sent and no client trial has been completed by this model.

BEFORE OFFERS
- Confirm drafter cost and available hours, reviewer cost and availability.
- Complete and review 3 permissioned sample exercises. Record every activity.
- Choose a repeat-work pricing policy using actual costs; disclose it before trial.

DIRECT PROSPECT TEST
- Qualify 60 independent direct prospects with a relevant next drawing package.
- Balance architecture/interior-design firms across three arms: $0, $25 and $49.
- Randomize 20 prospects to each arm before sending offers.
- Keep the same scope, source requirements, review, turnaround and follow-up.
- Track all offers and refusals. Do not remove unsuccessful prospects from results.
- Start with at most 10 completed trials total, at most 4 in any arm; if a capacity
  limit is reached, record subsequent requests as deferred, not declined.
- Small scope: one clean drawing excerpt or contained redline task estimated at
  no more than 1.5 production hours, plus review, PM and correction allowance.
- Quote larger or incomplete tasks separately. One introductory task per firm.
- Free work has no favorable-review requirement and does not buy a testimonial.
- Upwork prospects: use portfolio evidence and a funded paid test instead. Do not
  route an Upwork prospect off-platform to obtain a free-work arrangement.

LIMITS
- Existing additional-cash ceiling: $950 for the first month.
- Separate experiment limit: $650 economic acquisition cost including allocated
  existing staff and sales time, or 30 team delivery hours, whichever occurs first.
- These overlap; the experiment cap is not additional to the cash ceiling.
- Record actual costs. Reduce the number of trials if the cap is reached earlier.

DECISION
- Free-task acceptance is not willingness to pay.
- Compare qualified replies, offer-to-trial conversion, offer-to-paid-follow-on
  conversion, total labor, realized contribution and acquisition payback.
- Do not assume that free and paid offers have equal conversion rates.
- Aim for 2 clients buying follow-on work with positive measured contribution,
  with at least 1 buying again. These are management checkpoints, not proof from
  a statistically powered experiment. A small test cannot identify a sure winner.
- If there are no paid follow-ons, review quality, segment, trust and scope before
  increasing free volume. No paid response after 10 samples justifies changing
  the test; it does not establish that true conversion is zero.
