# Electric Distribution Drafting and Records

**November 24, 2026 · Niche 06**

Electric utilities pay for record drawings, staking-sheet production, and GIS cleanup, with the strongest public evidence inside broader engineering or system-migration contracts. Skarion’s best opening is a redline-to-record batch for an approved utility engineer or GIS prime, where the buyer can supply standards, data and a reviewer.

## Offer and buyers

Sell **distribution record drawing and asset-update production** to distribution engineering primes, public-power GIS integrators, or municipal utility and cooperative engineering leads with an explicit outside-vendor route. Convert approved field markups and design redlines into client-format CAD record sheets or staging-layer GIS edits. Reconcile drawing revisions and identifiers against the client's source packet and produce a traceable exceptions list for review. Where a design owner supplies approved layout and instructions, prepare the draft staking sheets, work-order graphics, and associated schedules to template. Field staking, switching decisions, electrical calculations, design changes, and final release stay with the utility or its designated qualified professionals. The work here concerns electric distribution records. Telecom attachment clearances, loading and make-ready are a separate offer.

Repeat triggers include service extensions, feeder conversions, undergrounding, completed construction work orders, and record cleanup in advance of data migration. Software migration can produce a significant one-time backlog. The recurring target is the subsequent flow of construction record updates. Esri retired ArcMap on March 1, 2026 and its October 2026 electric as-built workflow notes that verified field data can take weeks to months to reach GIS. Those are timing and workflow signals. Buyer awards below establish spending. ## Decision and pilot

**Keep as a focused second-wave offer or add it to a utility CAD/GIS campaign once a prime confirms a separable backlog.** Published purchasing is credible. The narrowest opportunity is closeout and data hygiene for an existing design owner, where a production team can increase throughput using that owner's approved source materials. Stand-alone price, annual production volume, qualified subcontract supply and approval for Skarion's delivery model are unverified. A single proposal for a specialist prime contract cannot resolve those questions.

Test a fixed-scope batch of **one completed work order, up to five record sheets, and a small associated sample of electric asset edits**. The prime provides approved redlines, formats and required attributes. Skarion returns marked revisions, traceable field changes, a reviewable GIS staging file and an exception register. Start with a de-identified or approved dataset, confirm access terms and software versions, and ask the prime to name the person releasing changes to its utility. Track time by production and review, first-pass acceptance, unresolved exceptions, and whether the same buyer has a following month's batch. Quote from timed inputs, review and rework assumptions. The larger system-integration awards provide no comparable per-sheet or per-asset price.

The highest-value next evidence is one named prime that discloses a funded, recurring production backlog, the exact vendor eligibility for that scope, and how many existing suppliers can turn it around to the required standard. The Fairmont, JEA and Ocala records identify concrete procurement routes to test without treating awarded or closed records as open jobs.
