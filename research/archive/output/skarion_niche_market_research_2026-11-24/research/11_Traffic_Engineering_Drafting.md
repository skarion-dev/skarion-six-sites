# Traffic engineering drafting

**November 24, 2026**

**Reachable demand 3/5, medium confidence. Supply tightness 3/5 for the sampled engineering-prime channel, medium confidence. Exact production-subcontract supply is unknown. Accessibility is medium with a licensed transportation partner and matching CAD standards.** Public buyers repeatedly procure signal and pavement-marking design. An Oklahoma prime itemized two traffic-design subconsultant scopes with substantial CAD hours, giving Skarion a credible route to test whether similar primes need flexible production support.

## Offer and buyer

Sell a defined batch of CAD sheets to a licensed transportation prime or a qualified traffic subconsultant. Start with a signing and pavement-marking plan from the engineer’s approved geometry, decisions, and redlines. Deliver the native DGN or DWG, plotted PDF, revision log, and a quantity-table reconciliation using the prime’s template. Signal-plan edits can follow once a prime confirms its design direction, access, and review process. Keep site verification, warrants, signal timing, engineering design decisions, final plan approval, and sealing with the responsible licensed team.

The best recurring buyer is an engineering firm with on-call assignments, successive plan submissions, or repeated intersection or corridor packages. The prime and its customer need to permit the workshare and Skarion’s delivery model before files move. Virginia illustrates two concrete checks. VDOT’s signing and marking manual specifies the relevant drafting conventions and MicroStation/OpenRoads cell libraries. Virginia’s seal rule addresses direct control, personal supervision, and the contract relationship when unlicensed personnel prepare regulated work. Confirm the project’s versions, access, and professional responsibility with its prime. [VDOT traffic engineering manual](https://www.vdot.virginia.gov/doing-business/technical-guidance-and-support/technical-guidance-documents/traffic-engineering-design-manual/) · [VDOT CADD manual](https://www.vdot.virginia.gov/doing-business/technical-guidance-and-support/technical-guidance-documents/cadd-manual/) · [Virginia seal rule](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title18/agency10/chapter20/section760/)

## Entry decision

**Validate selectively through primes.** Several small prime competitions were thin and transport teams visibly buy CAD-heavy workshares. Evidence remains too limited to place traffic drafting in the top five on low supply alone. The highest-value additional observation is a prime with recently awarded work confirming a recurring sheet queue, approved subcontract delivery, and delayed or unfilled requests from incumbent providers.

Test a paid **three-sheet signing and pavement-marking package** based on buyer-redlined source sheets and owner-supplied standards, with one consolidated revision round. Time setup, drafting, review, and rework independently. Ask the buyer to score references and file structure, plot match, and quantity-table traceability against an accepted example. Repeat orders, review burden, and incumbent lead times would make supply and margin more measurable. Where work is federally funded, start from the owner’s applicable project standards, including the current MUTCD and agency requirements. [FHWA MUTCD, 11th Edition with Revision 1](https://mutcd.fhwa.dot.gov/kno_11th_Editionr1.htm)
