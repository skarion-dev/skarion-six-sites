# Floodplain GIS and FEMA mapping support

**Research cutoff November 24, 2026.** Agencies pay for flood-risk geospatial datasets, terrain development, map production and QA, and several engineering contracts repeat this work through on-call assignments. Skarion’s most plausible entry is a tightly scoped production work order from a qualified engineering prime with authority over the inputs and approvals.

| Measure | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Reachable demand | **3/5, medium confidence.** A separately purchased geospatial project, ongoing mapping awards, and multiple tenders with identifiable GIS tasks show repeat potential. Purchase and frequency of the exact desktop subcontract remain unclear. |
| Qualified supply tightness | **3/5, medium confidence for bundled public engineering.** Four observed public competitions had 2, 4, 6, and 7 submissions or statements of interest. **Unknown for remote terrain, map, and QA production.** Established engineering and geospatial firms have demonstrable capabilities and known agency roles. |
| Skarion accessibility | **Medium-low, conditional on workshare approval.** Start with prime-managed inputs, a demonstrated ArcGIS deliverable and named reviewer. Some buyers restrict subconsultants or require advance approval and relevant qualifications. |

## Offer and buyers

Start with a **paid GIS production batch** for one reach or a small, agreed watershed area. The prime supplies accepted elevation data and engineer-approved layers, coordinate and vertical references, mapping schema, and templates. Skarion packages cleaned data, map exhibits, metadata and source lineage, and an exceptions log. Acceptance can cover spatial reference and vertical units, topology and attribute completeness, raster consistency, required metadata, and traceability to the prime-approved outputs. The prime owns hydrologic and hydraulic analysis, flood extent interpretation, engineering certification, and regulatory submission. [New Jersey task descriptions, pp. 14–16](https://nj.gov/treasury/dpmc/Assets/Files/advertisements/project-term-advertisements/RFP_Flood_Plain_Mapping_TC_011_FINAL_9_8_26.pdf) · [FEMA Risk MAP standards training](https://emilms.fema.gov/is_2101/groups/84.html)

Early buyers are project managers and GIS leads at state Cooperating Technical Partner (CTP) engineering primes, county stormwater consultants, and regional planning contractors. Common purchase triggers include a newly funded watershed study, updated LiDAR, corrections after engineering review, or a production release for a map revision. Geography matters through state buyer requirements and federal service territories. Location alone does not establish eligibility for a buyer’s information systems, approved vendor pool, or regulated work. ## Commercial decision

Keep floodplain GIS as a **second-wave or cross-sell test under qualified GIS and stormwater primes**. Documented competitions cover bundled teams, and the closest separate purchase combined mapping with substantial geospatial analytics. Supplier availability for the narrow desktop offer remains unmeasured. Advance it if an engineering or geospatial prime can name a funded release with a recurring production queue and authorize the subcontract and data handling.

The most useful next evidence is a dated backlog or approved work-order line for a past-due GIS package a prime would actually purchase. Ask how many GIS-ready reaches are expected over the next quarter, what their current production lead time is, which acceptance checks drive rework, and how many approved teams can start by the required date. A test should price one reach with a source register, documented input checks, sample maps and GIS data conforming to the engineer’s accepted outputs, and one review cycle. Record delivery and reviewer hours before committing to additional batches.
