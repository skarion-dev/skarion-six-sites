Summary
Survey Drafter Needed for Topo & Boundary Linework (Carlson / AutoCAD), Ongoing

We’re a busy land surveying firm on the East End of Long Island, New York, and most of our work is high-end residential in the Hamptons. Our workload keeps growing, and we’re looking for an experienced survey drafter to work with us on a long-term basis.

What you’ll be doing

You’ll take our field data and turn it into clean, finished boundary and topographic surveys. That means linework, labeling, contours, spot elevations, annotation, symbols, title blocks and general drawing cleanup. Our licensed surveyors handle the boundary determination. We need someone who can take that and produce a polished, correct drawing that follows our standards.

We’ll give you everything you need to get it right: our CAD standards, layer and block library, checklists, and finished examples of our work.

Who we’re looking for

A lot of hands-on experience drafting boundary and topographic surveys. This is the main requirement.
Enough survey background to understand what you’re drawing, not just trace it.
Strong skills in AutoCAD and/or Carlson Survey. Civil 3D is a plus.
High standards. You check your own work before you send it, and revisions should be rare.
Clear, responsive communication in written and spoken English. If something in the field data looks wrong, you ask about it.
A plus: experience with subdivision layouts, site plans or other civil drafting.

Room to grow

We’re starting with survey drafting, but we want someone who will be with us long-term. If you do good work, we’ll teach you more of the business: site planning, subdivision layouts and civil engineering drafting.

How we’ll start

Shortlisted candidates will complete a paid test drawing. If it goes well, we’ll start with steady work and increase hours from there.

To apply, tell us:

How many years you’ve drafted boundary and topo surveys, and for which countries or regions
What software you use day to day
One or two samples of survey drawings you’ve done (with client information removed)

Start your proposal with the word “Contour” so we know you read this post.

2. What land surveying is, and why drafting exists
A land survey is the measured and researched description of land and features on or near it. A survey crew
collects observations with instruments such as total stations and GNSS. A licensed surveyor considers
measurements together with record documents, monuments, adjoining evidence, and applicable rules to
determine or confirm boundaries. A drafter translates the supplied results into a readable plan; they should
understand the information enough to recognize discrepancies, but they do not determine a legal boundary on
their own. New York's consumer guidance distinguishes boundary, topographic, construction-layout and other
surveys.
Boundary survey: shows the licensed surveyor's conclusion about property limits and their relation to
observed improvements. Deeds and tax parcel polygons are useful background but are not a substitute for the

Topographic survey: shows terrain and existing physical features. A spot elevation is the measured height at
one location; a contour connects locations of equal elevation inferred from a surface. Breaklines are surveyed
lines along abrupt changes of grade, such as a curb, wall or ditch edge. Without them, surface triangulation can
cross a feature and produce believable-looking but false contours. Buildings, pools, drives, trees, fences, utility
structures and water features need clear linework and symbols. The datum and units must be identified if
known.
Why the finished plan matters: a buyer or lender needs to understand the lot and improvements; an
architect needs reliable existing conditions; a civil designer needs grade and drainage context; a builder needs
dimensions and site constraints; permitting staff need legible exhibits. The same point file can be unreadable to
all of them until it is cleaned, classified, checked and annotated. In coastal Long Island, flood, wetland, drainage
and site planning questions raise the cost of misreading an elevation or an improvement. A Hamptons firm's
published service descriptions illustrate these downstream uses; the firm has not been confirmed as the
Upwork client.
The legal handoff: New York defines professional land surveying broadly and places professional
responsibility and signing/sealing of survey documents with the licensed surveyor. In this project the client says
its licensed surveyors perform boundary determination. Skarion should present itself as survey CAD drafting
support, with the client's licensed professional supplying/reviewing the boundary and taking responsibility for
any certified deliverable. Do not market Skarion as a New York licensed land surveying practice without the
required authorization. Sources: NY definition and licensing, NY practice guidance, NY practice FAQs.
3. Read the data before drawing: the production chain
1. Request and scope. Determine whether the task is boundary, topo, as-built or a combination; get the
drawing template, plot scale, delivery format, point format, existing base survey and field notes. Record who
is the responsible licensed surveyor.
2. Records and boundary decision. The surveyor reconciles deeds, filed maps, monuments and other
evidence. Receive their approved boundary geometry, corner IDs and annotations. If their information
disagrees with field evidence, raise a question.
3. Field collection and control. Crews observe control and detail points. A common exported coordinate list
contains point number, northing, easting, elevation and description. Raw observations/field notes may be
separate. Check how the source defines axes, units and vertical reference.
4. Point import. Verify the incoming column order and that the points plot at the intended size and location. A
transposed easting/northing or feet/meters mistake can still draw a plausible picture. Keep the original file
untouched and log processing edits.
5. Field-to-finish linework. Codes such as EP (edge of pavement), FNC (fence), BLDG (building) and GND
(ground) map points into lines, symbols and layers. Codes are office-specific: the sample CSV supplies explicit
point sequences, and the team must configure its own code table or connect points manually. See Carlson's
explanation.
6. Existing-ground surface. Use the right ground shots and 3D breaklines, exclude non-ground objects and
constrain the surface extent. Inspect triangles at edges, buildings, walls, swales and sharp grade changes. A
contour is an interpolated product, not an extra observation.
