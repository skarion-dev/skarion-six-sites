# BIM and MEP production

**Demand 4/5, medium-high confidence. Open marketplace supply tightness 2/5, medium-high confidence. Approved private subcontractor availability is unknown. Accessibility is medium for a bounded package under a US trade or design lead.** Three comparable US-buyer postings open to worldwide freelancers each showed 20–50 proposals. The channel with a better fit for Skarion is repeat HVAC or mechanical model production for a contractor with an existing coordination workflow and a named reviewer.

## Offer and buying route

Sell a defined batch to a US mechanical contractor’s detailing manager, a general contractor’s virtual design and construction (VDC) team, or an MEP consultant with approved redlines. Use the buyer’s current Autodesk Revit model, linked trade files, marked-up approved routing, families, and issue schedule. Deliver the revised native model, updated sheets and schedules within scope, an issue log, and a Navisworks clash report against buyer-supplied tests where applicable. Weekly coordination drops, returned trade comments, phase releases, and fabrication or record-model deadlines create repeat batches. Confirm project BIM Execution Plan (BEP) responsibilities, required level of development by model element, naming, coordinates, and software versions before quoting. [National BIM Standard BEP](https://nibs.org/nbims/v4/bep/) · [National BIM Standard project requirements](https://nibs.org/nbims/v4/pbr/)

There are three different buyer decisions. A design team may buy **model or sheet updates from decided geometry**. A trade contractor may buy **detailing, coordination, or shop-drawing production**, which can require fabrication databases and field judgment. An owner may buy **campus scanning, BIM policy and modeling**, often requiring site access, data controls and a wider technical team. Skarion’s initial product is the first decision, plus authorized trade-detailing components when the buyer retains review and release. Independent system sizing, code compliance, reroute approval and fabrication release remain with the responsible lead.

## Campaign decision

Keep BIM and MEP production as a targeted adjacent offer. The best first sale is a **paid one-zone, one-discipline model update** for a mechanical firm with a confirmed week-by-week backlog. Agree to a bounded redline count and affected sheets, the delivery format and Revit release, a usable test model, clash tolerance, and a first-pass acceptance check. Send a revised model and sheets plus an issue log that identifies unresolved routing or field conflicts for the buyer to decide. A buyer-approved change set and verified model delta are better proof of capacity than an image-only Revit portfolio. [BEP acceptance and information exchange guidance](https://nibs.org/nbims/v4/bep/)

Before market entry, validate Revit and Navisworks Manage competency, access to the buyer’s collaboration platform, US trade conventions, and data and geography terms. Confirm whether a scope uses buyer families or fabrication-part libraries and who authorizes any field-driven changes. Have the buyer’s designated design or trade reviewer sign off before fabrication or external issue. Compare quoted scope with observed production and reviewer hours, buyer acceptance and paid repeat blocks.

**Evidence that could change the rank.** Ask approved contractors how many vendors met their last milestone, how many ready-to-model zones or issues are queued, and whether they can share the inputs with Skarion’s delivery team. Measure actual days to obtain quotes and prior rework under that customer’s standard. Two or more comparable paid packages with repeated vendor availability problems and an attainable local or trade-lead arrangement would strengthen the case for moving this higher. The observed public proposal bands support demand, while exact approved supplier capacity stays unknown.
