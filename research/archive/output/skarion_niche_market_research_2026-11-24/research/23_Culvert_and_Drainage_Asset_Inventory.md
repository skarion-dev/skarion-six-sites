# Culvert and drainage asset inventory

Culvert register cleanup and GIS updates have a credible regional subcontract market. The strongest case is established public works programs that already collect records and photos and need periodic GIS delivery, alongside engineering primes holding larger contracts that include this work.

| Dimension | Rating |
| --- | --- |
| Reachable demand | **3/5, medium confidence.** Multiple county and regional buyers purchase inventories and follow-on asset management. The narrower desktop production component is explicit in several scopes, and recent records rarely show it purchased separately. |
| Qualified supply tightness | **3/5 for the broader inventory and inspection channel, medium confidence. Unknown for the exact desktop subcontract.** Buyer records include one historic zero-response posting, two- and three-offer wider engineering competitions, and a nearby current asset mapping case with ten proposals. |
| Skarion accessibility | **Medium through an approved civil, GIS, or inspection prime or eligible regional buyer with usable existing records.** The prime or owner supplies field findings and retains condition ratings, survey acceptance, hydraulic conclusions, and sign-off. |

## Offer and repeat trigger

Offer an editable culvert register from supplied field forms, photos, CAD as-builts, tabular records, and GIS layers. Preserve asset IDs, source provenance, photo links, coordinates, dimensions, approved condition codes, and the distinction between recorded and suspected attributes. Deliver a data dictionary, schema and coordinate-system checks, duplicate reconciliation, and an exception list for the approving owner. Highway department asset managers, regional councils, and civil or transportation primes are the intended buyers. Acquisition routes include an approved prime’s subcontract and a bounded direct task order where permitted. Repeat work can follow scheduled field rounds, repairs or replacement, transfer of inspection photos, new record drawings, or an annual reporting deadline. New Mexico DOT has a current schema and requires a data reassessment with the project as-built package where its specific provision applies. [NMDOT Culvert Asset Management Program](https://www.dot.nm.gov/culvert-asset-management-program-camp/)

## Skarion decision

**Regional first test or active alternate for the top five.** The offer has repeat purchases, a dated Connecticut reporting trigger, and one credible failed procurement, plus relatively strong evidence that existing data can be used. Reachable demand is narrower than bundled inspection figures suggest. Prioritize funded Northeast and Great Lakes programs where an approved prime or COG can show a dataset backlog, confirm who accepts the result, and identify the providers already available. Keep it as the smaller-structure inventory specialization within a broader drainage or GIS campaign when both opportunities originate from the same buyer.

Propose a paid **25–50-record, one-jurisdiction GIS pilot** with a client-supplied schema and source set. Deliver an importable layer, source coverage report, photo link check, missing attributes and duplicates register, and handoff to the owner’s GIS or engineering lead. Scope pricing from the number and format of records and manual reconciliations. Ask the prime how many records are waiting, how many suitably experienced providers are already approved and have capacity, and whether it buys this block independently, by subcontract, or through internal staff. The most useful next evidence is a recurring work order for the exact post-field GIS task with real quote response or lead-time data.
