## Pursuit sequence

1. **Start with Broadlinc and HR Green.** Qualify who owns the next drawing release and whether already-approved subcontractors cover it. Offer a paid, fixed-scope production batch from client-approved design basis and field inputs. Deliver up to five plan sheets, splice and quantity schedules, a native CAD or GIS handoff and a source/exception register. Keep licensed design, field verification, permitting decisions and acceptance with the authorized team.
2. **Use the open buyer gates.** Otsego’s two-project timetable favors a team already holding the full documents. For Long Beach, qualify a Class A bidder and verify all live portal notices, approval terms and access restrictions. Treat its security-network inputs as controlled by the prime and owner.
3. **Track documented commercial follow-through.** Confirm the remaining batch, who can approve a sub, when authorized inputs arrive and which cost center can fund it before forecasting revenue. Track first-pass acceptance, prime review time, quantity conflicts and whether the same prime places a second paid order. Buyer proposal counts and contract awards measure prime-level competition. The number of approved vendors competing for a production batch remains unknown.
